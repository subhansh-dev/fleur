## 1. Fine water mist extinguishers (ISS → Earth)

- NASA developed **fine water mist technology** for spacecraft fire suppression — the Mist
  experiment (NTRS 20040053582, "The Water Mist Fire Suppression Experiment: Mist Preliminary
  results") and multiple-orientation discharge studies (NTRS 20100001376) established how fine
  water sprays extinguish fires where gravity doesn't settle droplets onto the flame.
- Physics that transfers to Earth: droplets evaporate in the flame zone, cooling it and displacing
  oxygen locally — **evaporation, not gravity**, does the work. That's why fine mist works on
  ordinary Class A/B fires with far less water than sprinklers.
- Challenge-video quote (SME Xiaoting Miao): "fine water mist extinguishers originally developed
  for use on the ISS have practical application here on Earth."
- Dashboard beat: one insight card + one citation chip. Clean, credible, 20 seconds.

## 2. Smoke detection improvements

- The SAME experiment family produced the definitive dataset of **microgravity smoke particle size
  distributions** for five spacecraft materials (Kapton, Teflon, silicone rubber, cellulose/lamp
  wick, Pyrell foam) — the basis for next-generation detector design, with dual-moment detection
  recommended (SAME final report).
- Earth spin-off framing: particle-size-aware detection benefits industrial cleanrooms, cargo
  holds, tunnels, and anywhere "dust vs smoke" discrimination matters (SAME explicitly worried
  about discriminating smoke from large dust particles).
- Commercial smoke detectors (First Alert-class) were flown and characterized in SAME — a direct
  consumer-hardware connection.

## 3. Firefighter gear & protective clothing

- Challenge-video quote (SME): NASA technology has led to improvements in "industrial smoke
  detection and firefighter gear, including protective clothing, air tank, etc."
- The NASA gap list (2021 tech-dev report) shows the mirror problem: **current crew clothing is
  flammable even at ISS atmosphere** — enriched-O2 clothing is a named NASA capability gap
  (2021-2906-TX06). Material-screening science (NASA-STD-6001, BASS-II fabric tests, Nomex
  characterization 20250010975) transfers both ways: spacecraft screening methods inform protective
  apparel testing on Earth.
- Wildland-urban interface angle (optional stretch): water-mist + clean-agent suppression research
  is relevant to enclosed-space firefighting (submarines, server rooms, EV cabins).
